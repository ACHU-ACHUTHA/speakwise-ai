# Present Perfect Tense Guide

## Overview
The Present Perfect tense connects the past to the present moment. It is used when an action happened in the past, but the exact time is not specified or the result is important right now in the present.

## Formula & Structure
- Positive: Subject + have / has + Past Participle (V3)
  - Example: "I have finished my homework."
  - Example: "She has visited London twice."
- Negative: Subject + have not (haven't) / has not (hasn't) + Past Participle (V3)
  - Example: "They haven't called me yet."
- Question: Have / Has + Subject + Past Participle (V3)?
  - Example: "Have you ever tried Indian food?"

## Primary Uses

### 1. Life Experiences (No specific time mentioned)
Use present perfect when talking about experiences in your lifetime.
- "I have seen that movie before."
- "He has lived in Japan."

### 2. Recent Actions with Present Results
- "I have lost my keys." (Result: I don't have my keys right now.)
- "She has just arrived." (Result: She is here now.)

### 3. Action Started in Past and Continuing in Present
Often used with **for** (duration) and **since** (starting point).
- "I have known Achu for 5 years."
- "We have lived here since 2020."

## Common Comparison: Present Perfect vs. Simple Past
- **Simple Past**: Specific completed time in the past (yesterday, last year, in 2010).
  - "I visited Paris last year." (Specific past time)
- **Present Perfect**: Unspecified time or connected to present.
  - "I have visited Paris." (Experience in my life)
